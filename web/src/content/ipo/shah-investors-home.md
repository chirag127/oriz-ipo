Shah Investor’s Home is currently open for bidding, with the issue running from 28 to 30 September. The grey-market premium is around 9.0% of the upper band, or ₹15, implying an unofficial market expectation of roughly a 9% listing gain from the ₹167 price band. That signal needs to be treated cautiously. GMP is an unofficial, unregulated, thinly traded and highly volatile indicator, and the premium can evaporate before listing. It therefore indicates current grey-market sentiment rather than a dependable forecast of the eventual listing price. The stated estimated listing level of ₹182 corresponds to an 8.988% gain, broadly consistent with the quoted GMP, but neither figure changes the fact that the grey-market signal can move materially before listing.

The subscription book is visible and shows overall demand of 15.27x. The mix is notable: QIB subscription stands at 5.51x, NII/HNI demand at 42.12x, and retail subscription at 9.34x. On these figures, demand is being driven much more strongly by the NII/HNI segment than by QIBs or retail investors. In market-structure terms, that makes the book look more concentrated in HNI/NII participation than in institutional demand, while retail participation is also substantial. The 42.12x NII/HNI figure can be consistent with leveraged HNI participation, although the subscription data alone does not establish how much leverage is actually being used. Similarly, the QIB figure provides evidence of institutional participation but does not by itself establish the degree of institutional conviction.

The issue size is ₹90.17 crore at a price band of ₹167. This is an SME issue, so its structure differs from a larger Mainboard offering. SME issues are generally smaller, can be more thinly traded, involve higher lot values and lower liquidity, and carry higher trading risk; Mainboard issues are generally larger and more liquid. For an investor assessing Shah Investor’s Home, that structural distinction matters alongside the subscription and GMP data. The immediate decision window is straightforward: the issue is currently open and therefore currently biddable, with the stated opening and closing dates of 28-30 September. It has not yet reached the listing stage.

Based on the supplied score and titles, the apparent reviewer lean is mixed rather than clearly one-sided, with the discussion appearing oriented substantially toward listing gains and near-term IPO outcomes rather than a purely long-term fundamental assessment. These are reviewer opinions, not established facts about the issue.

Taken together, the available data may be of interest to applicants focused on a potential listing gain and comfortable with GMP volatility and the liquidity characteristics of an SME issue. Investors who need liquidity, are more risk-averse, or would treat GMP as a guarantee of listing performance should be cautious about relying on that indicator. The current book shows strong headline subscription, particularly from NII/HNI investors, while the market-implied listing signal remains unofficial and subject to change before listing.
