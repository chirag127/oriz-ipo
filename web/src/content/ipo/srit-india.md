SRIT India is currently open, with the issue running from 28-30 September and a stated issue size of ₹218.40 crore at a price of ₹130. The grey-market premium of about 23.9% of the upper band, or ₹31, points to an unofficial market expectation of a listing price around ₹161 and therefore a potential listing-gain signal of roughly 23.85%. That said, GMP is an unofficial, unregulated, thinly traded and highly volatile indicator. It can change sharply or evaporate before listing, so it should not be treated as a firm indication of the eventual listing price or return.
The subscription book is visible and shows overall demand of 58.2 times the shares on offer. The split is notably uneven: QIB subscription stands at 6.19 times, versus 176.67 times for NII/HNI and 37.15 times for retail. On the figures provided, demand is therefore being driven much more strongly by the NII/HNI segment than by QIBs, with retail also showing substantial participation. This is a different demand profile from one led predominantly by institutional bidding; the very high NII/HNI figure indicates particularly strong participation from that category, while the retail multiple also points to significant retail interest. The ₹218.40 crore issue is an SME issue, rather than a larger Mainboard offering. Structurally, SME stocks generally have smaller issues, thinner trading, higher lot values and lower liquidity than Mainboard listings, which can translate into higher trading risk.
The investor decision window is straightforward: the issue is currently biddable, with the stated opening and closing dates of 28-30 September. The available YouTube review sample is mixed rather than uniformly positive, with an aggregate review score of 0.49/1 across five videos. The titles repeatedly use “Apply or Avoid,” “Final Verdict,” and “Final Decision,” while another focuses on “Business, Financials & Key Risks.” This suggests reviewer opinion is leaning toward an application-versus-avoidance debate rather than a uniformly constructive view. The presence of GMP-focused titles and the stated estimated listing level also indicates that listing gains are an important part of the discussion, although at least one review explicitly frames the assessment around business, financials and key risks. These are reviewer opinions, not independently established facts about the issue.
In practical terms, the available data is most directly relevant to investors considering the issue for a listing-gain-oriented approach and who are comfortable with GMP volatility and the structural liquidity characteristics of an SME listing. Investors who require liquidity, are more risk-averse, or are inclined to treat the grey-market premium as a guarantee should be more cautious in interpreting the signals. The key observable features are strong overall subscription, particularly from NII/HNI and retail bidders, a much lower QIB multiple, and a positive but unofficial GMP signal, alongside the higher-liquidity constraints inherent in an SME issue.
